kill (62)

Strategy: determinized · dispatch: typed · discussion (new)

Facts as of 403e350e9e82 · prose checked 2026-11-20 at 403e350e9e82

In short

Natively, a signal sent to a process is taken by whichever suitable thread the host kernel happens to pick, at a moment host scheduling chooses, and whether the target still exists can change under the sender. Hermit settles who the target is, whether it is alive, and which thread takes the signal inside the sender's own scheduled turn, against its model of the guest's threads, so the same schedule sends the same signal to the same thread. Sends to a whole process group or to every process are refused instead of modeled, apart from one unmaskable case and signal-zero probes, and on a backend that does not model signal targets a send to a multithreaded process is refused. Known gaps: 8.

Determinism strategy

What is nondeterministic natively. kill sends a signal to the process, process group, or set of processes its pid argument names, and for a process-directed send the kernel chooses the receiving thread from those that do not block the signal, so the receiver, the moment of delivery against the target's own execution, and whether the target has exited, been reaped, or changed its mask in the meantime all follow host scheduling.[linux] Even the existence answer is racy natively: a check with signal zero and a later real send can disagree because the target died between them, and permission is judged from credentials at the instant the kernel runs the check.[linux]

What Hermit does. Detcore determinizes kill by deciding the send in the sender's scheduler turn against its own thread tree, in the guest's virtual pid namespace, before anything reaches a kernel: typed dispatch reaches a handler that resolves a positive pid to the process it names, answers existence from the set of live threads plus the recorded exit state, and only then routes an unambiguous send onward.[c1][c2][c4] A process with no live threads is answered from that record alone: an exited process its parent can still wait for counts as found and the signal is dropped, a gone process fails with ESRCH, and a single live thread is the receiver, while several live threads need the scheduler's receiver choice below and signal zero needs none.[c3] That choice replays the kernel's preference for a thread that wants the signal, read from the masks and dispositions the schedule holds, and where Detcore cannot prove the choice — a backend that does not model signal targets (LiteInst, SaBRe and DBT; only ptrace, e9patch and KVM do), an unknown mask, an exec in flight, a receiver that could take the copy out of turn, a signal the instrumentation keeps for itself, or a run that records or replays — the send is refused by name through the unsupported-operation policy instead of letting stopped threads be picked by host timing (G2).[c14][c20] One case needs no choice and is forwarded unchanged: a send to the sender's own multithreaded process, on a backend whose kernel signal state is the guest's own, when the schedule already forces which thread the kernel admits, with the sender holding its turn until it takes the copy.[c13] Before a positive-pid send goes onward it also passes an admission step, which on KVM refuses by name a send to another process, or to the sender's own multithreaded process, that would kill or stop it by default or whose receiver is in a state the scheduler cannot place in turn (G6).[c21] The unmaskable signal is settled differently again, and the settlement depends on the backend: on ptrace and e9patch a SIGKILL to another process retires every thread of that process at the sender's turn, so no receiver choice remains, and a SIGKILL to the caller's own process takes that same retirement with the sender included; on KVM a SIGKILL to the caller's own task is fenced in the turn as a process exit, its exit state recorded and its robust lists walked where no host exit walks them, while a SIGKILL to another process is refused at admission (G6); on DBT the send goes to the lowest live thread and the kill notice retires the group; and on SaBRe and LiteInst, where the target has several threads, such a send is refused for want of a modeled receiver (G2).[c15][c9][c22] Sends that do reach a kernel go in that same turn, and on a backend that must name the receiving thread Detcore rewrites the process-directed send into a thread-directed one to the chosen thread, because guest pids there are Detcore's own identifiers, not host task ids.[c19][c11] Two cases are refused before any effect: a send of an in-guest reserved signal to another live process, and, under the in-guest delivery gate, a stop signal (the stop signal always, the other stop-class signals at their default disposition, the caller's own process included), each with EPERM and a warning, while a caught or default-fatal send to a thread blocked in a kernel-run call under that gate ends the whole run (G7)[c18]; Hermit's own preemption signal instead takes the appropriated-signal refusal in G2 when its target has several threads.[c23][c24] A guest-sent SIGALRM can likewise be refused with EPERM on in-guest LiteInst while a guest handles that signal (G8).[c25]

Interface fidelity. The pid forms are honored exactly where the model can name the receivers: a positive pid names its process (any live thread's id names the process, and the leader is acted on), signal zero sends nothing and answers existence from the same live-thread and zombie record as a real send, and the most negative pid value fails with ESRCH before any lookup, as the kernel answers it.[c6][c7][linux] A pid of zero, a negative pid naming a process group, and minus one naming every signalable process are refused by name with no fallback inside the call (G1), with two exceptions: a signal-zero probe in any of those forms is forwarded unchanged and the kernel's answer is kept by record and replay, and an unmaskable SIGKILL to a group named by a pid below minus one (which may be the caller's own group) is forwarded where guest tasks carry real namespace pids, after the scheduler is told in the same turn, its members retired at that turn where the backend lets the scheduler retire a SIGKILL at the kill turn (ptrace and e9patch), although KVM may refuse that forwarded group send by name.[c8][c12][c7][c28] The error order follows the kernel's where Detcore answers itself on its model paths (on a refusal path an invalid signal number to a multithreaded target can instead meet the refusal, G2): a target that is gone fails with ESRCH before the signal number is judged, an invalid signal number to a found zombie fails with EINVAL and sends nothing, and a receiver Detcore cannot choose fails with ENOSYS under the allowance for unsupported operations while a fail-closed run stops instead.[c3][c10][c14] Ordinary credential permission is not re-decided in Detcore: a forwarded send keeps the kernel's own permission answer unchanged, the only permission failures Detcore itself returns are its named gate refusals above and the alarm-signal refusal in G8, and a send Detcore answers from its model alone — a zombie target, including an existence check with signal zero — returns success or EINVAL without the permission check Linux runs even for signal zero, so such an answer never reports a permission failure (G5).[c19][c23][c24][c3][c10][linux] Two sends succeed without delivering anything, as they do for a namespace init on Linux: a signal naming one of Hermit's own tasks is dropped and answered with success, and a signal the kernel drops for the caller's own namespace init is forwarded unchanged for the kernel to drop.[c17][c6] A caller in a pid namespace the guest made itself names that namespace's pids: positive pids are translated through the scheduler's record (an unknown one fails with ESRCH), group forms with a real signal from such a caller are refused rather than translated, and on a backend that sends with container-namespace ids a nested send is refused by name (G4).[c16][c6] The call never blocks, so there is no EINTR path, and its thread-directed siblings that name a thread and group directly take the same turn discipline without this page's receiver choice.[c4]

Record & replay. The call is subscribed in both run and record/replay modes (code facts in the Appendix).[c2] A send that reaches a kernel goes through the record-or-replay path, while every answer Detcore gives from its thread tree — existence, the zombie drop, and each named refusal — is regenerated at the same schedule point from the same model state.[c19][c3][c7] The one narrowing is the receiver choice for a multithreaded target, which record and replay do not reproduce: such a send is refused there rather than forwarded on a guess (G2).[c14]

Known gaps

  • G1 (unimplemented): a send with a real signal to the caller's own process group, to another named group, or to every process the caller may signal is refused by name, because neither group membership nor each member's receiving thread is modeled; the exceptions are a signal-zero probe in those forms, which is forwarded unchanged, and an unmaskable SIGKILL to a group named by a pid below minus one on a backend whose guests use real namespace pids; on KVM that forwarded group send may instead be refused by name. A guest has no fallback inside the call and must signal its targets process by process.[c12][c8][c28]
  • G2 (unimplemented): a process-directed send to a process with several live threads is refused by name wherever the scheduler cannot prove the receiving thread the kernel would pick — a backend that does not model signal targets (LiteInst, SaBRe and DBT), an unknown mask, a thread that could take the copy out of turn, an exec in flight, an appropriated signal, any record or replay run, or, under schedule replay, a self-send the recording forwarded or a receiver the recorded schedule runs first — returning ENOSYS where unsupported operations are allowed and stopping a fail-closed run, and on those refusal paths an invalid signal number meets the refusal instead of Linux's EINVAL. Single-threaded targets, signal zero, and the forced own-process forward are unaffected; SIGKILL is unaffected only where a backend retires it at the kill turn (ptrace, e9patch and DBT) or fences it as above, and there is no guest fallback for a refused multithreaded send.[c14][c3][c20][c26][c22]
  • G3 (by design): without sequentialized threads there is no turn to decide in, so an ordinary send goes to the host kernel as written and its delivery timing is not made deterministic, while every group or broadcast send with a real signal is refused for host safety, because the guest's process group there is inherited from Hermit's and a forwarded group send could reach Hermit and the processes that started it.[c5]
  • G4 (unimplemented): pids from a pid namespace the guest created are only partly served: positive pids translate through the scheduler's record, but that namespace's group ids are not translated and its group sends with a real signal are refused, and on a backend that rewrites process sends with container-namespace ids every nested send is refused by name. A guest inside such a namespace can still signal individual translated processes where the backend forwards with the caller's own numbers.[c16][c6]
  • G5 (unimplemented): a send to a zombie target, including a signal-zero existence check on one, is answered from the thread-tree model alone and skips the credential permission check Linux runs before answering even signal zero, so a caller Linux would refuse for its credentials instead receives the model's success answer and no permission failure (the model's ESRCH and EINVAL answers are Linux's own and do not diverge); a real signal naming one of Hermit's own tasks is likewise dropped with success from the model without that check. Forwarded sends keep the kernel's own permission answer, and there is no guest fallback that recovers the missing check on the model path.[c3][c10][c7][c27][linux]
  • G6 (unimplemented): on KVM, a send to another process is refused by name at admission when it is a SIGKILL; a send to another process, or to the sender's own multithreaded process, is likewise refused when its default action would stop the receiver, when its default action would end it (unless the receiver waits for the signal in rt_sigtimedwait), or when the receiver's state cannot be placed in turn, where Linux would deliver it. An admitted send can still end the run later with HERMIT_KVM_SIGNAL_CLAIM_REFUSED if the chosen receiver then waits where it cannot take the copy, exits or blocks the signal first, or a parked observation contests the copy. There is no guest fallback inside the call.[c21]
  • G7 (unimplemented): under the in-guest delivery gate, a caught or default-fatal send to a thread blocked in a kernel-run call ends the whole run with a named stopping refusal instead of interrupting that call as Linux would; no guest fallback recovers the delivery.[c18]
  • G8 (unimplemented): on in-guest LiteInst, while a guest handles SIGALRM, a guest-sent SIGALRM to a process that handles it, or from a sender without the serial grant, is refused with EPERM where Linux would deliver it; other backends take the ordinary path.[c25]
